Scrap — Privacy Policy

Effective: September 13, 2026
Contact: contact@nemofactory.co.kr

In one line. Scrap has no server that receives your data. There is no sign-up and no sign-in, and we never ask for your name, contact details or any other personal information. Your photos are read on this device only, and the app deletes from your library exactly the ones you decided to delete — your photos are never sent anywhere. The only places the app touches the internet are one status file and ads (sections 4 and 5).

1. Information we collect

None. We do not collect your name, contact details, email, date of birth or any other personal information. The app has no accounts, so there is no sign-up or sign-in at all.

We run no server that receives your data. There is nowhere for your information to accumulate on our side.

2. Photos and library access

Scrap lets you go through the screenshots and photos piled up in your library one card at a time, so it needs access to your photo library.

  • Reading — to list your screenshots and photos and show them on the sorting and folder screens.
  • Deleting — the app deletes from your library only the shots you swiped to “delete”, and only after you confirm at the end of a round. On iOS the system shows one more confirmation dialog right before anything is deleted. Deleting a folder also deletes the photos inside it from your library; the app tells you this before it does it.
  • No saving — the app never creates new photos in your library and never modifies the originals. iOS also carries an “add to library” permission string, because iOS keeps that entry alongside the photo permission; the app has no saving feature, so it is never used.
  • Limited access is fine — you may allow only selected photos instead of the whole library. The app works the same way; it simply shows only what you allowed.
  • No transmission — your photos are never uploaded to us, to advertisers, or anywhere else. Lists and previews are drawn entirely on the device.

We do not analyse the content of your photos. There is no face detection, no text recognition, nothing that identifies what is in a picture. We do not read capture locations (EXIF) either — the app never requests location permission. It does not use the camera or the microphone.

3. Stored on your device only

  • The sorting ledger — a database on your device recording what you decided about each shot: the photo identifier (a value your library assigns inside that phone), the decision (deleted · kept · filed to a folder), the folder number, the size of deleted shots, and when you decided. It does not contain the photos themselves. The identifier only means something on that one device.
  • Folders — folder name, icon, order and creation time. The names are the ones you type in.
  • App settings — how many shots per round, the language you chose, and dark mode (system · light · dark).
  • Ad-related records — the time of an ad tap (so a mis-tapped ad slot rests for a while), the time an ad was shown (so the same slot is not filled again immediately), and the ad settings received from the status file (section 5). None of this identifies a person.
  • Running totals — how much space you have freed in total, and whether the rating prompt has already been shown. The total is stored separately so that it survives when you clear folders or the sorting log.

All of the above stays on your device. It is never sent to us and we cannot read it. Deleting the app deletes all of it.

4. Requests the app makes

These are the only places the app touches the internet. Your photos and your sorting decisions go to none of them.

  • Status file (beautiful-tulumba-77ca83.netlify.app) — at launch the app reads a single public JSON file holding the minimum supported version and the ad settings. Everyone receives the same file and we attach nothing of yours to the request. As with opening any web page, the hosting provider (Netlify) may record your IP address in its server logs. If the read fails, the app simply opens as usual.
  • Ads — see section 5.
  • Opening this policy · rating the app — tapping these in Settings opens your browser or the store app, which may leave the same kind of access record. While you use the app, the operating system may also show its own rating prompt once; that prompt is displayed and answered entirely by the operating system, and we never learn who rated the app.
  • Contact us — the app does not send anything; it only opens your mail app (section 9).

5. Ads

Every feature of this app is free and ads are the only way it pays for itself. Nothing is locked behind an ad, and there are no rewarded ads.

There are two ad placements.

  • A banner at the bottom of the home, library, settings, sorting, summary and folder screens.
  • One interstitial shown after a sorting round has been applied. It never appears while you are sorting.

Who serves the ads
Ads are served through Unity LevelPlay (formerly ironSource), a mediation service that picks which ad network fills each request. The networks currently connected are below; this list may grow or shrink.

  • Unity Ads · ironSource (Unity Technologies)
  • Liftoff Monetize (Liftoff Mobile, Inc.)

Their policies: Unity Privacy Policy · Liftoff Privacy Policy

What they collect
To serve ads and measure them, the providers above may read the following from your device and take it to their own servers. We never receive, store or view any of it.

  • Advertising identifier — IDFA on iOS, AAID on Android. You can reset or block it in your device settings at any time.
  • Device and app information — device model, OS version, language, country, app version, screen size, network type.
  • IP address — passed along with the ad request; it may be used to estimate an approximate (city-level) location.
  • Ad interactions — how many times an ad was shown and tapped.
  • Ad SDK diagnostics — whether ad requests succeeded and how long they took; performance and error metrics of the ad code itself.

Your photos and sorting decisions are never passed to advertisers. What is in your library, what you deleted, what you named a folder — none of it reaches them. The app has no location feature; the “approximate location” above is what an advertiser infers from the IP address carried by the ad request.

iOS — the tracking prompt
On iOS, the system asks once whether the app may track you. You can decline and still use every feature, and you will still see ads — they simply will not be personalised. If you decline, the advertising identifier (IDFA) is not passed to the ad providers. You can change this at any time in iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.

What we declared on the App Store privacy card
Apple groups the values above into its own categories. Scrap declares Device ID · Advertising Data · Product Interaction · Other Usage Data · Coarse Location · Performance Data · Other Data — all of it the same data listed above. None of it is linked to your identity (there is no account to link it to), and the items declared as used for tracking are the advertising identifier (Device ID) and the ad interaction records (Advertising Data, Product Interaction, Other Usage Data). Photos are not on that list, because they never leave your device.

No ads on Android right now. The Android build ships without ad settings, so no ad requests are made and no advertising identifier is read. We will update this section if that changes.

Protection against mis-taps
When an ad is tapped, that slot rests for a while so a brushing finger cannot open ads over and over. The tap time used for this stays on your device (section 3).

Ads can be switched off remotely
Whether ads are shown at all also depends on the status file in section 4, so ads may turn off or on without you reinstalling the app. While they are off, the providers’ code never starts and none of the values above are read.

6. Transfers abroad

We do not move your information abroad. In the cases below, however, your device communicates directly with servers outside your country, so some information may be processed there.

  • Recipients and data — Unity Technologies (Unity LevelPlay · ironSource · Unity Ads) and Liftoff Mobile, Inc. (Liftoff Monetize): the values in section 5 (advertising identifier, device and app information, IP address, impression and tap records). Netlify, Inc. (status-file hosting): your IP address. No personal information and no photos are included in either case.
  • Countries — the United States and other countries where these providers host their servers.
  • When and how — over the network, at launch (status file) and when an ad is shown.
  • Purpose and retention — ad delivery and measurement, file delivery. Retention follows each provider’s own policy (links in section 5).
  • How to opt out — decline tracking or reset your advertising identifier and it will not be passed on (section 5). Deleting the app stops all transfers.

7. Analytics and crash reporting

The app contains no analytics or crash-reporting SDK. We cannot tell which photos you deleted or which screens you spent time on.

We do see the anonymous statistics the App Store and Google Play compile for developers (installs, device mix, crash counts). Those come from the stores, cannot identify a person, and are not collected by the app.

8. Sharing with third parties

We do not share or sell your personal information. We hold none to begin with.

Ad delivery and measurement are, however, carried out by the providers below. We do not hand data to them; their code inside the app reads the values in section 5 directly and sends them to their own servers.

  • Unity Technologies (Unity LevelPlay · ironSource · Unity Ads)
  • Liftoff Mobile, Inc. (Liftoff Monetize)

9. Information we receive from contact messages

The contact form in the app only opens your mail app; the app itself sends nothing to us. Whether to send is your decision, made in the mail app.

If you do send it, we receive your reply address and what you wrote. We use it only to answer you and fix the problem, and delete it once that is done.

10. Retention

We collect no personal information, so we have no retention period. Everything in section 3 lives on your device and disappears the moment you delete the app. Contact emails (section 9) are deleted once handled.

11. Security

This app runs no server that receives your data. Not creating a place for a leak to happen is this app’s security measure. Every request the app makes uses an encrypted connection (HTTPS).

12. Your rights

We hold no information about you, so there is nothing for us to disclose, correct or delete on request.

  • Sorting history — the ↺ next to “Sorted” in Settings clears the whole ledger. The running total of space freed is not cleared there; deleting the app removes it along with everything in section 3.
  • Deleted photos — photos removed from your library stay in “Recently Deleted” in the Photos app for a while and can be restored from there. That is handled by the operating system, not by this app.
  • Advertising identifier — reset it or turn off personalised ads as described in section 5.

13. Children

This app is not directed at children under 14, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children.
