OFFME — Privacy Policy

Effective: September 30, 2026
Contact: contact@nemofactory.co.kr

In one line. OFFME has no accounts, no sign-in, and no server that stores your information. Because it is an app that blocks the apps you choose, it reads which apps to block, which app is open right now, and how many steps you walked today on your device. Those values are used only on your device and never leave it (sections 3, 4 and 5). The only internet requests the app makes are checking an app status file, store payments, and, on Android, a one-line payment notice at the moment a purchase completes — all listed in section 9. There are no ads and no analytics tools.

This app does not monitor you. OFFME helps you manage your own device. It has no feature for looking into someone else's device, and it never sends your usage to a parent, an employer or any third party.

1. Personal information we collect

None. We do not collect your name, contact details, email, date of birth, address, payment information or any other personal information. There is no account feature, so there is no sign-up or sign-in at all.

The sex, age, height and weight you enter in "About me" (section 2) and your step count (section 5) are stored only on your device and are never sent to us. The Android payment notice (section 9) carries nothing that identifies a person, and it is not kept after the notice is shown.

2. What is stored on your device only

The values below are saved in the app's private storage on your device (a settings file) and never leave the device. On iOS, the values that the blocking screen, the widget and the Screen Time extensions need are also written to a shared container used only by this app (App Group). That container is also on your device, and other apps cannot read it.

  • Apps to block — on iOS, only an opaque token given by Apple; we cannot read which apps it refers to (section 3). On Android, the package names of the apps you chose (e.g. com.google.android.youtube).
  • Time locks — a timer's name, start time and end time; a routine's name, days, start/end times and apps to block; and the time of any scheduled turn-off or deletion.
  • Step locks — the lock's name, the daily step goal, the apps that open once the goal is met, and the time of any scheduled turn-off.
  • About me — the sex, age, height and weight you pick yourself, entered when you first create a step lock (you can skip it) or under "About me" on the Me tab. Used only to calculate a recommended step goal, distance walked and calories burned; never used to decide whether apps are locked.
  • Records — finished timers (start and end times), steps walked each day and whether the goal was met, total time locked, times you ended a lock early, days kept without a fine, and the day you first opened the app. Used only to show them back to you on the Stats and Me tabs.
  • Open for a bit — when the current open window ends, and whether today's free open has been used.
  • Purchase status — whether you are subscribed, how many unused passes you have, and the store transaction numbers kept so that the same purchase is never applied twice. No payment details such as card numbers.
  • Settings — uninstall protection on/off (iOS), your chosen language, and the date the rating prompt was shown (section 11).

None of these values is sent to us, and we cannot read them. Deleting the app deletes all of them (section 12).

3. Device information read for blocking — the most important section

OFFME blocks the apps you choose, so it has to know what to block and what is open right now. How this works differs by platform.

iOS — the app uses Apple's Screen Time frameworks (Family Controls · Managed Settings · Device Activity), in which Apple deliberately hides app information from us.

  • The screen for choosing apps is a system screen drawn by Apple, and what you choose comes back as opaque tokens. We cannot read which apps you picked.
  • Applying and lifting locks, the blocking screen shown when you open a locked app, and routines switching on and off at set times are all handled on the device by Apple's system and this app's extensions.
  • When uninstall protection (on/off on the Me tab) is on, apps cannot be deleted from the device while a lock is active. This also works only on the device, through Screen Time settings.
  • As Apple requires, Screen Time data is never sent off the device, never shared with or sold to third parties, and never used for ads or analytics.

Android — the app reads the following directly on the device.

  • Installed app list — only apps with a launcher (home screen) icon, so we can show their names and icons for you to choose from. We do not use the QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES permission that would reveal hidden apps.
  • The app in the foreground — a monitoring service checks at short intervals which app is frontmost and, if it is a blocked app, draws a blocking screen over it. To do this it reads app-switch events through Android's Usage Access permission.
  • We do not read what you do inside an app — text you type, screen contents and messages are not accessed and we make no attempt to access them. We do not use an accessibility service.

4. What never leaves your device

None of the values read under sections 3 and 5, or stored under section 2, ever leaves your device. Specifically, the following are sent to no one, including us:

  • the installed app list, the apps you chose to block, and the app in the foreground
  • your locks, routines, step goals and their times
  • your step count, sex, age, height and weight, distance and calories
  • every record on the Stats and Me tabs

The app contains no code that uploads these values anywhere.

5. Step count

A "step lock" keeps the apps you chose locked until you reach today's step goal. For this the app reads the number of steps you walked today.

  • iOS — it reads only the step count counted by the device's motion sensors (Core Motion). iOS asks for the "Motion & Fitness" permission, and the prompt reads "Used to count your steps to unlock your apps and show your walking history."
  • Android — it reads the device's step sensor, for which it asks for the Physical activity (ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION) permission.
  • The step count is used only to check the step goal, unlock apps, keep your records and show the widget, and it never leaves the device. The app does not access health data stores such as Apple Health (HealthKit) or Google Health Connect. It does not read your location.
  • If you decline the permission, time locks still work; step locks simply tell you that steps cannot be counted.

6. Permissions — what and why

iOS

  • Screen Time (Family Controls) — the only way Apple provides for blocking apps. Requested only for managing your own device, never for supervising another person's (a child's) device through Family Sharing. Without it, blocking does not work.
  • Motion & Fitness — to count steps for step locks (section 5). Time locks work without it.

On iOS the app requests no other permission. It does not request notification permission either.

Android

  • Usage Access (PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS) — to check which app is in the foreground. Without it, blocking does not work.
  • Display over other apps (SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW) — to draw the blocking screen over a blocked app. Without it, blocking does not work.
  • Foreground service (FOREGROUND_SERVICE · FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE) — so monitoring keeps running after you close the app. A notification stays visible while it runs.
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS) — to show that ongoing notification. Blocking still works if you decline.
  • Run at startup (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED) — so blocking resumes by itself after the device restarts.
  • Physical activity (ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION) — to count steps for step locks (section 5).
  • Internet and billing (INTERNET · BILLING) — for the requests in section 9 and Google Play payments.

Android treats "Usage Access" and "Display over other apps" as special permissions that you turn on yourself in the settings screen, and you can turn them off there at any time. Turning them off stops blocking.

7. What the app does not use

The app does not access, and does not request permission for, the camera, microphone, photos, location, contacts, call logs, messages, calendars or health data (HealthKit · Health Connect). It does not read advertising identifiers (IDFA · Android advertising ID), and it never shows iOS's tracking permission (ATT) prompt.

8. Ads and analytics

There are no ads. No ad network (SDK) is included, so no ad requests are sent. No analytics or crash-reporting tools are included either. We do not collect how you use the app.

9. Every request the app sends out

The following is the complete list of cases in which the app sends a request over the internet. Blocking and step locks themselves work on the device without the internet.

  • App status file — when the app starts, it reads one small file on our website (hosted on Netlify) to check whether your version is too old and must be updated. This request only downloads the file and carries no values about you. As with opening any web page, the hosting server's logs may record the connecting IP address. If there is no connection, the check is skipped and the app works as usual.
  • Store payments — showing prices, buying something and restoring purchases talk to the App Store · Google Play on your device. This is handled by Apple's and Google's payment systems; our server is not involved (section 10).
  • Payment notice (Android only) — the moment a purchase completes on Android, the app sends one request to our server (gwansang-server.onrender.com, hosted on Render) to tell the developer "one was sold". It carries only the app name, platform, product name and ID, amount and store order number — nothing like your name, contact details, device identifiers, step count or blocked apps. The server forwards it as one line to the developer's messenger (Telegram), uses the order number only briefly so the same notice is not sent twice, and does not store it. If the request fails, the purchase is not affected. This request is never sent on iOS.
  • Links you tap yourself — "Privacy policy" (this page, GitHub Pages), "Terms of Use" (Apple's standard terms page), "Manage subscription" (the App Store · Google Play subscription screen) and the store link on the update screen open your device's browser or store app. From there, that site's own policy applies.

10. Payments

The app sells the following. All are optional; without buying anything you can still use the basics (timer locks, one routine or step lock, and one free "Open for a bit" per day).

  • OFFME Pro (monthly · yearly subscription) — create unlimited routines and step locks. It renews automatically at the same price unless canceled at least 24 hours before the period ends; cancel in the App Store · Google Play subscription settings.
  • Open for a bit (consumable) — opens locked apps for 10 minutes.
  • End now (consumable) — turn off or delete a routine right away instead of waiting out the 24-hour notice, or end a running timer right away.
  • Payments are processed by Apple's App Store and Google Play. Payment details such as card or account numbers never reach us, and we cannot see them.
  • All the app receives from the store is what was bought, the local price to display, and a transaction number, used only on the device to apply the purchase (section 2).
  • Restore Purchases asks the store again for what you own. Our server is not involved here either.
  • Payment and refund records are kept by Apple and Google under their own policies.

11. Contact and rating prompts

Contact us at the bottom of the Me tab only opens your mail app. The app does not send anything on your behalf; you decide whether to send. If you send an email, its contents (what you wrote and your reply address) reach us; we use them only to reply and delete them once the inquiry is answered.

Rating prompt — after you have used the app for a while, it occasionally shows a prompt asking for a star rating. This is a system prompt shown by Apple or Google, and any rating or review you leave is handled by the store. Nothing is sent to us. The app only records on the device the date the prompt was shown.

12. Retention and deletion

We keep no information about you, so there is nothing to ask us to delete (contact emails are deleted after replying, as in section 11). You can delete the values on your device (section 2) yourself.

  • Deleting the app deletes every value in section 2.
  • On Android you can also reset it under Settings → Apps → OFFME → Storage → Clear data.
  • On iOS you can revoke Screen Time access under Settings → Screen Time, and step-count access under Settings → Privacy & Security → Motion & Fitness.

13. Sharing, processing and sale

None. We collect no personal information, so there is nothing to share with or entrust to third parties. We sell no information. Payments are handled directly by Apple and Google under their own policies.

14. International transfer

No personal information is transferred abroad. However, the requests in section 9 reach servers of overseas providers — the app status file goes through Netlify (USA), and the Android payment notice goes through Render (USA) and Telegram. These requests carry nothing that identifies a person.

15. Children

OFFME is not directed at children under 14, and we do not knowingly collect children's personal information. We collect no personal information from anyone to begin with. Screen Time permission is requested only for managing your own device, never for a parent supervising a child's device.

16. Your rights

Under applicable privacy laws you have the right to request access, correction, deletion or suspension of processing. However, we hold no information about you, so there is nothing to request. You can delete the values on your device at any time as described in section 12. For questions, contact contact@nemofactory.co.kr.

17. Security

We run no server that receives your information, so there is none to leak from a server. Values on the device are kept in the app's private storage and in a shared container used only by this app, which other apps cannot read. The app contains no code that sends your usage, step count or body information off the device.

18. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we will update the effective date on this page. The revised policy takes effect on the day it is posted here.
